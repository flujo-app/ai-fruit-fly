What's real, and what's hand-made?

Real

  • The wiring. All 138,639 neurons and 15,091,983 connections of an adult female fruit fly brain, mapped from electron microscope images by the FlyWire team (2024).
  • The brain model. Each neuron follows the simple "charge up, then fire" rule from Shiu et al. (Nature, 2024), with their settings. In lab tests, that model predicted real fly behaviour well.
  • Which neurons are used. Senses feed into real, named cell types (eye cells LC10a, LC4 and LPLC2, sugar and bitter taste cells, antenna touch cells). Movement is read from real output cells: the giant fibre (escape), MN9 (feeding), MDN (walking backwards) and steering cells such as DNa02.
  • The decisions. Steering toward food, escaping the swatter, feeding on sugar but not on bitter, and grooming after a puff of air all come from signals flowing through the real wiring.

Hand-made

  • The body. The 3D fly, its leg movement and its flight path are animations, not a physics model. The fly's real "spinal cord" isn't part of the FlyWire map.
  • The eyes, tongue and antennae. How strongly each sense fires is a simple formula based on what's around the fly.
  • Turning signals into movement. Which output cells mean "turn", "feed" or "jump", and how strongly, was chosen by testing the model. The right side's steering signal is boosted ×1.5 to balance both sides.
  • Extras. Hunger, gentle random wandering when nothing is in sight, and staying on the cloth are simple rules. Smell is left out, because it sets off far too much of the brain at once in this model.
